Walls

Water grips a wall over half a millimetre, far less than a cell. Grip stops the whole first cell, so films hang on walls (a 1.5 cm film fell 38% as far as it should). Slide lets the wall reflect the water and puts back the friction the law of the wall gives (80%). Sliding walls take less energy out, so the water stays livelier.

Half-full cells inside the liquid

Pressure never fills a surface cell that has liquid all round it, so pools show holes. Filling them slowly closes the holes; it moved the dam-break front by about one point.
